He Is the Moon. I Am the Tides. She Is the Sunshine Standing in the Way.

5 min readEssay

She is personified sunshine. I don't say that as a metaphor I reached for — I say it because it's the most literal true thing I know about her. She wakes up bright. She narrates her own joy out loud, unprompted, over breakfast, over nothing. She builds things — actual sandcastles, blanket forts, elaborate worlds made of couch cushions — with the specific confidence of a kid who has never once doubted that the world around her is safe enough to build in.

I used to say he was the tides. It felt true from a distance — the way he comes in fast, pulls back, comes in again, unpredictable in timing but not in shape, the same pattern arriving over and over like weather instead of choice. It was easier to say that. It kept the destruction abstract, natural, nobody's fault, the way you don't blame the ocean for being the ocean.

But it isn't true. He is not the tides. He is the moon.

He is the pull. Something in his dysregulation reaches across the space between us and tugs, and I feel it happen in my own body before I've decided to let it — a pressure building low and out of reach of my own control, the same way the moon doesn't touch the water and still moves the whole ocean anyway. He doesn't hit the sandcastles. He has never once, in any literal sense, touched anything she built. He just pulls. And I am the one who rises.

I am the tides. I am the thing that actually rolls in, actually reaches the shoreline, actually goes higher than it was supposed to and takes the sand with it on the way back out.

By the time I've noticed how far I've come in, I'm already there — already loud, already sharp, already spilling into a room that had nothing to do with what actually happened between him and me, and there she is, mid-build, watching the whole thing come down without ever having done anything to deserve the tide reaching her at all.

That's the part I can't get out from under some nights. He is the pull. I am the flood. And she — sunshine, uninvolved, radiant and entirely innocent — is just standing in the path of something that was never actually about her, left holding wet sand where a castle used to be, trying to figure out what she did wrong when the honest answer is nothing, it was never you, it was never even really him, it was the distance I let the water travel once I felt the pull.

I don't know how to stop being pulled. I don't think that part is fully mine to control, any more than an ocean gets to opt out of a moon. But I am starting to understand that the distance the tide travels — how far past the normal line, how much it takes with it, whether it reaches her sandcastles at all — that part might be mine. Not the pull. The distance.

Some nights I rebuild the sandcastle with her before bed, saying nothing about why it needed rebuilding, just building. She never asks why. She just builds. Personified sunshine doesn't hold a grudge against the tide — she just starts again, brighter, like she hasn't yet learned that some things, once knocked down enough times, are supposed to stop being rebuilt so easily.

I hope she never learns that. I hope I figure out the distance before she does.

If this hit close

If you've felt the pull turn into a tide that reaches shorelines that never asked for it — the pull isn't the part you can fully control. The distance the water travels might be.
