The Mental Load Pattern Called "I'll Figure It Out"

And a script to actually break it

5 min read Free template Updated this month
It sounds like flexibility. It's actually the mental load quietly re-routing back to you — every time — because asking twice feels like more effort than just doing it yourself.
The pattern

Look for it and you'll see it everywhere in an overloaded day: you ask for help. You get a soft no, a maybe, a "let me know," an "I can try, but—." And instead of pushing back, you say it — out loud or just in your head — "okay, I'll figure it out."

Why the ask fails

Most requests for help fail before they're even finished, because they're phrased as soft, deflectable, and vague — "could you help with the kids' stuff," "can you get things moving," "do you think you could help." Every one of those has an easy out built right in.

The fix: specific, timed, non-deflectable asks

Name the task, name the time, and ask for confirmation — not just hope. A vague ask lets someone default to "I can try." A specific one requires an actual yes or no.

Instead of: "Can you help me get things moving, I'm running late?"
Try: "I need you to get [child] dressed and downstairs by 7:15. Can you do that specific thing?"
Instead of: "Do you think you'll be home early today?"
Try: "I need you home by 5:30 today because I have [specific thing]. Can you confirm that works, or tell me now if it doesn't so I can find another plan?"
Instead of: "Could you help with the groceries like we said?"
Try: "Groceries are in the car. I need them in and put away in the next 15 minutes while I start dinner. Can you do that now?"
