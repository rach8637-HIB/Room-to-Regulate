The System Wasn't Built for "Both" — Here's How to Navigate It Anyway

5 min readFree guide
Giftedness can mask a disability. A disability can mask giftedness. Either way, the kid often ends up formally recognized as neither.
What "twice-exceptional" (2E) actually means

A 2E kid is gifted and has a diagnosable disability — ADHD, autism, a specific learning disability, or another qualifying condition — at the same time. That combination isn't rare in the way it's treated: research estimates suggest 2–5% of gifted students (source: Davidson Institute, 2026) also have an unaddressed disability, and many more are never identified as either.

Why the system misses so many of these kids

Gifted identification and special education identification are, in most districts, two separate systems that don't talk to each other. That creates a structural blind spot: giftedness can mask a disability (a kid compensates well enough that the struggle reads as "just needs to try harder"), and a disability can mask giftedness (a kid's struggles are visible enough that no one looks past them to notice the ability underneath). Either way, the kid often ends up formally recognized as neither — just "inconsistent."

Questions to bring to an evaluation, to get both sides seen
Ask: "Can we evaluate for giftedness and for a possible learning disability or ADHD at the same time, rather than sequentially?"
Ask: "What would it look like for his giftedness to be masking a disability, or his disability to be masking his giftedness — has that been considered specifically?"
Ask: "Can we see the actual discrepancy between his ability testing and his achievement testing?"
Ask: "Is our state's gifted mandate full or partial?"
A note on the paperwork itself

2E kids often qualify for accommodations under an IDEA-based IEP or a Section 504 plan for the disability side — but the gifted side rarely shows up in either document unless a parent specifically asks for it to be included. Nothing about IDEA prevents this. Most schools simply don't think to offer it unless asked.

The takeaway

This system was not built with "both" in mind. That doesn't mean you can't build a version of it that works — it just means you'll likely be the one doing the building.
