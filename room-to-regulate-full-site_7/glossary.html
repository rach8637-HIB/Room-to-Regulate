The Room to Regulate Glossary

3 min read

This site references a lot of shared vocabulary — some clinical, some systemic, some shorthand for things we keep coming back to. This page collects the terms in one place

HCBS (Home and Community-Based Services)

Medicaid-funded support — respite care, in-home behavioral help, personal care aides — that lets someone stay at home instead of in an institution.

Why it matters: This is the main path to actual in-home help for the hardest hours of your day, but it's not guaranteed coverage — see HCBS Waiver.

HCBS Waiver

The specific mechanism (under Section 1915(c) of the Social Security Act) that lets a state offer HCBS at all. States get a fixed number of funded "slots," not open enrollment.

Why it matters: Meeting every requirement doesn't guarantee a spot — there's usually a waitlist, and it can be long.

Entitlement (vs. Non-Entitlement) Program

An entitlement program (like regular Medicaid) must cover everyone who qualifies. A non-entitlement program (like HCBS waivers) only covers as many people as it's funded for — everyone else waits.

Why it matters: This is the legal distinction behind why your regular Medicaid never has a waitlist, but HCBS almost always does.

IEP vs. 504 Plan

An IEP (Individualized Education Program) provides specialized instruction under federal special education law; a 504 Plan provides accommodations under civil rights law, without changing the curriculum.

Why it matters: Different legal footing, different levels of support — worth knowing which one actually fits your kid's needs.

Masking

Suppressing or hiding natural behaviors (stimming, dysregulation, sensory responses) to meet others' expectations, often unconsciously.

Why it matters: It's why a kid can be "no problem" at school and fall apart at home — the mask comes off where it's safe to.

Respite Care

Short-term relief care for the primary caregiver, sometimes covered through HCBS waivers.

Why it matters: It's one of the few forms of support aimed at you, not just your kid — worth knowing it exists even if access is limited.

2E (Twice-Exceptional)

A kid who is gifted and has a diagnosable disability at the same time — ADHD, autism, a specific learning disability, or another qualifying condition.

Why it matters: Gifted identification and special education identification are usually separate systems that don't talk to each other, so 2E kids are among the most under-identified in schools.

Full vs. Partial Gifted Mandate

Whether a state legally requires both identification and services for gifted students (full mandate), only identification (partial), or neither. Unlike special education, gifted education has no federal standard at all.

Why it matters: This single fact changes what you're legally entitled to request from your school district.

Mental Load

The invisible, ongoing cognitive work of managing a household — remembering, tracking, anticipating, coordinating — distinct from the physical tasks themselves.

Why it matters: It's the thing behind most of the "I'll just figure it out" moments this site talks about — a structural pattern, not a personal failing.
