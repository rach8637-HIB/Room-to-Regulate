Reference directory

Anyone can post a claim about Medicaid, Medicare, or disability services. Not every source is equally reliable – especially in today's political climate. This directory links to primary sources and established policy research organizations, organized by topic, so you can check something yourself in under a minute instead of trusting whoever shared it first.

A quick way to sort trustworthy from not: government sites (.gov), established nonprofit policy research organizations with public funding disclosures (like KFF or CBPP), and your state's own Medicaid/health agency are reliable starting points. Be skeptical of anything that asks for payment to "help you keep your benefits," anonymous social posts with no named source, and anything that can't tell you exactly which law or rule it's citing.
Medicaid — official & primary sources
Start here for anything about eligibility, work requirements, or your specific state's rules.
Medicaid.gov
The federal government's own Medicaid site — program rules, state-by-state information, official guidance documents.
KFF (Kaiser Family Foundation) — Medicaid
Nonpartisan health policy research, widely cited by journalists and policymakers across the political spectrum.
Center on Budget and Policy Priorities — Medicaid
Policy research focused on how budget and eligibility changes affect low- and middle-income families.
Medicare — official & primary sources
For plan changes, coverage questions, and Medicare Advantage specifically.
Medicare.gov
The official U.S. government Medicare site — plan comparison tools, coverage rules, enrollment periods.
Center for Medicare Advocacy
Nonprofit legal advocacy organization that tracks Medicare policy changes and helps beneficiaries understand their rights.
SHIP (State Health Insurance Assistance Program)
Free, unbiased, one-on-one Medicare counseling in every state — not selling you a plan, just explaining your options.
Disability rights & HCBS waivers
For questions about home and community-based services, waiver waitlists, and disability-specific protections.
Administration for Community Living (ACL)
Federal agency overseeing home and community-based services and disability supports nationally.
Medicaid Waiver Directory
A state-by-state directory of HCBS waiver programs, waitlist status, and application information.
Gifted & 2E education policy
For questions about your state's gifted mandate and identification requirements.
National Association for Gifted Children (NAGC)
Tracks state-by-state gifted education mandates and federal legislative activity.
Davidson Institute
Research and resources specifically on twice-exceptional (2E) identification and support.
ACA marketplace insurance
For current premium costs, subsidy eligibility, and enrollment.
HealthCare.gov
The official federal marketplace — actual current pricing and subsidy eligibility for your situation, not an estimate.
KFF Premium Tax Credit Calculator
Estimates your actual premium cost with your income, age, and family size — useful for budgeting before you enroll.
Vaccine policy & coverage
For checking what's actually changed vs. proposed, and what your specific plan covers.
CDC Immunization Schedules
The federal government's own current schedule — check this directly rather than relying on secondhand summaries.
KFF — Vaccine Policy Tracking
Nonpartisan tracking of exactly which coverage requirements changed, which didn't, and what's still contested in court.
Special education (IDEA / OSERS)
Your child's IEP rights are set by law, not by which federal office currently administers them.
IDEA — sites.ed.gov/idea
The federal government's official site for IDEA law and regulations, regardless of which agency currently handles day-to-day administration.
COPAA (Council of Parent Attorneys and Advocates)
Tracks changes to special education law and administration, and can help you find a special education attorney or advocate if you need one.
Food assistance (SNAP)
For current eligibility rules and to check your own exemption status.
USDA FNS — SNAP Eligibility
The federal agency's own current eligibility rules, including work requirement exemptions.
Center on Budget and Policy Priorities — Food Assistance
Tracks SNAP changes state-by-state as they roll out, in plain language.
Find what's still available where you live
National program changes matter most in how they show up locally. These are starting points to find what's actually reachable in your area right now, regardless of what's changed federally.
211.org
Free, confidential service connecting you to local resources — food, housing, childcare, healthcare, crisis support — by ZIP code. Call 211 or search online.
Eldercare Locator / Area Agencies on Aging
Though aimed at older adults, these regional agencies often maintain the most current local directories of caregiving and disability support services in your county.
Your state Medicaid agency
Search "[your state] Medicaid office" — your state agency (not a federal one) determines your actual local HCBS waiver waitlist status, provider directories, and appeal process.
Parent Training and Information Centers
A federally funded center in every state, specifically to help parents navigate special education and disability services locally — free, and not going anywhere with federal budget changes.
Legal help & direct advocacy
If you need to appeal a denial or need direct legal assistance, these aren't information sites — they're people who can actually help.
Legal Services Corporation — Find Legal Aid
Directory to free or low-cost legal aid organizations in your area for benefits appeals and disputes.
Disability Rights (state-by-state network)
Protection and advocacy organizations exist in every state — search "[your state] disability rights" to find yours.
