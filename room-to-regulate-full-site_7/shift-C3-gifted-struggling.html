Gifted and Struggling Isn't a Contradiction Schools Are Required to Notice

5 min readPolicy explainer

There's an assumption baked into a lot of "so smart, but—" conversations: that if a kid is really struggling, the school will catch it, and if a kid is really gifted, the school will notice that too. Neither assumption holds up, and the reason is clearly not individual teachers failing individual kids. It's structural.

There's no federal requirement to identify or serve gifted students

Unlike special education, which is federally mandated under IDEA, gifted education has no federal standard (source: NAGC Federal Legislative) update for identification or programming. It's entirely a state-by-state patchwork — 33 states have a full mandate (source: Renzulli Learning, 2026) 50-state research requiring both identification and services, 10 states (including Pennsylvania) have only a partial mandate, and the rest require neither.

Where that leaves 2E kids specifically

For a 2E kid, that patchwork becomes a genuine gap. The disability side of their profile is federally protected — evaluation and services are legally required if a disability is suspected. The giftedness side has no such floor. If a state has only a partial mandate, a school can be fully compliant with the law while never formally recognizing that a kid struggling with executive function is also operating well above grade level in other areas — because nothing requires them to look for it.

The gap here isn't that schools don't care. It's that the law never told them where to look.

What's actually moving

Some states are tightening this. Missouri passed a bill through its House (source: Missouri Independent, Feb 2026) in early 2026 requiring universal gifted screening by third grade, closing part of the "only if a parent asks" gap that lets 2E kids slip through. It's a state-level fix, not a federal one — but it's a real example that this gap is not fixed or unchangeable, just under-legislated almost everywhere.

One thing you can do

Check your own state's gifted mandate status before your next school meeting — full mandate, partial mandate, or none — since that single fact changes what you're legally entitled to request. If your state has no mandate or only a partial one, that's worth raising directly with a school board member or state representative.

"I'm a constituent and a parent of a twice-exceptional student. I'd like to know whether our state currently mandates gifted identification and services, and if not, whether there's any legislative movement to change that. I'd like to be kept informed and to support that effort if it exists."
