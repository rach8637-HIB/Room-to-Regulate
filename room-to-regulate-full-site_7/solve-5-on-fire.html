When Everything Is on Fire at Once

The 5-minute triage script for the moment dogs, dinner, and bedtime all collide

4 min read Free template Updated this month
You just walked in the door. Dogs are frantic. Bags are on the floor. Groceries are still in the car. This is the order — not because it's "right," but because it prevents the most damage.
STEP 1 Whoever is about to have an accident goes first

Dogs who haven't been out, kids who need the bathroom — biological urgency beats everything else on this list, no exceptions. Handle it before anything else, even if it feels like it's derailing your plan. It's not a derail. It's the actual first step.

STEP 2 Anything frozen, perishable, or alive gets handled next

Groceries that'll spoil, medication that's time-sensitive. Everything else on the list can wait ten more minutes. This can't. Bring it in, deal with the parts that'll actually go bad, and let the rest sit on the counter if it has to.

STEP 3 Pick ONE task to delegate out loud, specifically, right now

Not "can someone help." Assign it, by name, with a task attached.

Try: "[Name] — dog food. [Name] — bring in the bags by the door."
Not: "Can someone please help me with all this?"
STEP 4 Food happens before bath, always

A hungry kid in a bath is a losing battle you don't need to fight tonight. Feed first, even if it's imperfect, even if it's not what you planned for dinner. Fed and slightly off-plan beats on-plan and starving.

STEP 5 One thing on your list gets to die tonight — choose it now, on purpose

Something was not getting done tonight anyway. The only real choice you have is whether you decide that on purpose, now, or discover it in defeat at 9pm. Pick it. Let it go. That's not failure — that's triage, and triage is a skill, not a shortcoming.
