## Brand and product direction

Room to Regulate serves neurodivergent moms and moms raising neurodivergent kids. The working paths are Survive (recognition and essays), Solve (scripts and tools), and Shift (policy and collective action). The homepage prototype prioritizes a reader's immediate need over explaining the brand architecture. One quiet editorial image gives the site a human presence; the rest of the interface stays spare.

The child activities address a specific parent problem: finding a few usable minutes to read, write, or call. They should be offered beside longer Solve tasks, such as the IEP letter builder, with language like “Need a few minutes to do this? Set your child up first.” The product should not promise uninterrupted time or replace supervision.

## Partnerships and boundaries

The uploaded `partner-card-demo.html` is the source for native placement rules: a clearly labeled card after useful Solve content, one partner at most, never interrupting a Survive essay or a step-by-step script. The strategy keeps Shift, the policy tracker, verified resources, and the glossary sponsor-free. The new mockups extend this approach into optional branded content; this expansion needs an explicit editorial policy before use. Suggested treatment: label “Paid Partnership” at the top, retain editorial control, include a useful non-partner path, and keep sponsored video separate from member discussion. HelloFresh, Care.com, and Undivided are prospective examples only.

The audience sheet and sponsor material include population statistics and placeholders for first-party reach. Do not present them as validated Room to Regulate user behavior. Verify current rates, product offerings, partner claims, and all sponsorship language before a pitch or launch. Do not share individual tool inputs or member data with partners.

## Research and release sequence

1. Pilot 6–12 interviews using the research kit. Include moms who identify as neurodivergent, moms raising neurodivergent children, and moms who are both. Ask about recent real moments before testing branded concepts.
2. Try the child prototypes with a small, varied group of children and parents. Observe whether a child can understand the first step, use spoken prompts on the target device, pause, and seek help. Measure actual engaged time; the “about five minutes” labels are estimates.
3. Test the homepage with readers: ask what they expect from each choice and whether the first useful action is clear. Replace index links with direct tool links once those tools are published.
4. Review the IEP builder’s state-specific guidance with a qualified specialist before publishing. Check all claims in calculators and policy tools against current sources.
5. Only then refine sponsor packages and test optional sponsored content. Keep audience trust measures alongside clicks and completions.

## Open decisions

- Final age bands: 3–5, 6–9, and 10–13; the 10–13 child activity version has not been made.
- Whether the younger digital experience should use recorded narration instead of browser speech synthesis. Browser voices and file-preview behavior vary.
- Where the child activities live on the site and whether they remain fully free without email capture.
- Whether paid membership contains any sponsored video and, if so, how often, where it appears, and what members can access without it.
- Partner approval and editorial standards for branded content, separate from the existing native card policy.
